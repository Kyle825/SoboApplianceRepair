## 4) Products and Services

### 4.1 Initial scope

**Major appliance repair focus:**
- Laundry (washers/dryers)
- Dishwashers
- Cooking (gas + electric stoves/ovens)

> **Refrigeration:** Keep as “limited / selective” until the core four are dialed in. Sealed-system work adds tooling + time variability.

### 4.2 Service workflow (the “one-trip” engine)

1. **Digital triage:** collect model number + symptom + photos + any error codes  
2. **Pre-visit research:** pull tech sheet / service mode / common failure patterns  
3. **Curated parts prep:** bring high-probability parts for the scheduled appliance type/brand  
4. **On-site diagnosis:** schematic-first troubleshooting, measurement-driven confirmation  
5. **Firm written estimate:** labor tier + parts + any surcharges listed clearly  
6. **Approval required:** repair begins only after customer approval  
7. **Job-close QA:** verify function, check for leaks, confirm safe operation  
8. **Review request:** QR + follow-up message after completion (non-negotiable if growth is desired)

### 4.3 Repair-vs-replace guidance

Use a flexible, customer-friendly heuristic:
- “We’ll advise when repair is not economically sensible.”
- Keep options open (customers sometimes still choose repair).

## 5) Pricing, Billing, and Compliance

### 5.1 Pricing model (current structure)

- **Service call/diagnosis:** $85 **per appliance**, due at visit; applied to labor if repair is approved
- **Labor tiers after diagnosis:**
  - **Tier 1:** $125 (≤ 60 minutes)
  - **Tier 2:** typical $185–$295 (60–120 minutes)
  - **Tier 3:** typical $295–$425 (120–180 minutes)
- **Parts:** billed with a handling/markup policy; new OEM preferred; used/reconditioned labeled explicitly
- **Surcharges:** stacked unit +$50; emergency/same-day +$50 (when applicable)

### 5.2 Written estimate + approval process

- Provide a **written estimate** after diagnosis and **before starting work**
- Repairs proceed only after customer approval

### 5.3 Invoice detail + itemization

- Itemize parts (new vs used/reconditioned) and list any additional charges
- Include labor/time details consistently (even when using flat-rate tiers)

### 5.4 Sales tax clarity

- Communicate that parts may be subject to applicable Maryland sales tax when itemized.

### 5.5 Parts sourcing strategy (pre-launch task)

Goal: reduce delays, improve margins, and support one-trip completion.

**Plan:**
- Maintain **2–3 primary sources**:
  - a local/regional distributor (fast pickup / same-day)
  - an online wholesaler (breadth)
  - a retail backstop (for “must-have-today” parts)
- Set up supplier accounts pre-launch to:
  - get better pricing vs consumer retail
  - obtain faster availability info
  - reduce time spent shopping during the day
